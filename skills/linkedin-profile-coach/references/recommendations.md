# Recommendations

Help the person get LinkedIn recommendations: choose the writers, draft the requests and the person's own recommendations for others, and check each recommendation that arrives. You draft; the person reviews every text and sends it through LinkedIn by hand (`rules-5`, [hand-paste only](../SKILL.md#hand-paste-only)). Each step cites the rules it follows; [best practices](best-practices.md) indexes them.

Keep every draft clean:

- You know a colleague's name only if the person tells you. Workspace files name each writer by relationship, such as "manager, last position", and drafts use `[Name]` as the placeholder.
- Praise from a review enters a message only as a theme, never as a quote.
- All drafts go in `drafts/recommendation-requests.md`. Run the NDA pass from the [confidentiality procedure](confidentiality.md) over it before you hand any of them over.

## Ask

1. **Choose the writers with the person** (`recommendations-1`):
   - people who saw the person's work first-hand: managers, peers, people the person supported, partner teams;
   - someone who wrote the person a reference letter, since only recommendations sent through LinkedIn show on the profile (`recommendations-9`);
   - a client or an outside partner only after the person has checked it against their contract, since a recommendation shows who wrote it.

   Use the audit's list of received recommendations, or ask the person which ones they have. Each request covers the most recent position the two shared that the writer has not covered yet (`recommendations-6`), and at least one request covers the current or last position (`recommendations-1`). This skill suggests three to five writers as a start. That number is the skill's own; LinkedIn sets no limit on the total (`recommendations-6`).

   Done when the person has chosen the writers, each by relationship, with the position each request covers.

2. **Connect first.** Confirm with the person that every writer is a 1st-degree connection. The person sends a connection request now to anyone who is not, while work email and chat still reach them (`recommendations-2`).

   Done when every writer is a 1st-degree connection or has a connection request from the person.

3. **Find what each writer could speak to.** Ask the person what the two of them worked on and what this writer praised. Match the answers to approved evidence records, including the `recognition` records, which name who gave the praise by relationship. A point that no record holds yet needs a record first: the person adds it with `work-history-harvest` and approves it there, before any message cites it.

   Done when each writer has two or three concrete, clean points, each with its evidence ID.

4. **Draft one message per writer.** Each message:
   - reminds the writer of the work the two did together, in one sentence;
   - names the points from step 3 as things the writer could mention, with results where the evidence has them (`recommendations-3`). A figure from a self-reported record, or from the self-reported part of a mixed one, goes in only as a range or a qualitative result, and only if the person confirms the writer saw it;
   - offers a recommendation in return, unless the person has already sent one (`recommendations-5`);
   - leaves an easy way to say no.

   Keep it short. LinkedIn documents no length limit for the message; the form decides (`recommendations-4`).

   If the person wants endorsements too, add one line to the request, or draft a short message of its own to a 1st-degree connection who saw the work. It names two or three target skills that colleague saw in use, from the skills listed on the profile when the message goes out, since connections can endorse only those (`skills-9`). Present endorsements as one minor signal (`skills-8`).

   Done when every writer has a draft with two or three concrete points and `[Name]` in place of every name.

## Give in return

5. **Draft the person's recommendations for others.** A recommendation helps the colleague too, so an exchange suits both, and the person may send theirs first (`recommendations-5`). Each draft names two or three concrete things the person saw the colleague do, with results where the person knows them (`recommendations-3`). The colleague must be a 1st-degree connection (`recommendations-2`), and the person sends the text through LinkedIn's Recommend flow (`recommendations-5`). When the person endorses colleagues, they pick skills they saw those colleagues use (`skills-9`).

   Done when each recommendation the person wants to give has a clean draft.

## Hand over

6. **Hand over the drafts** once the NDA pass is clean. With each request, give the steps of LinkedIn's Request a recommendation flow, and ask the person to check the relationship, the position and their primary profile language before sending. The relationship cannot change once the request is sent (`recommendations-4`). Say why they paste every message by hand. Tell them when to send:
   - after the Experience entries each request needs are on the profile, so its position is there to choose (`recommendations-4`);
   - after the switch, when the person is making English the primary profile language, because requests go out only in the primary profile language (`recommendations-4`, `sections-2`);
   - one open request per writer at a time. A writer who does not answer may have declined, and LinkedIn does not report a decline, so the person can ask someone else (`recommendations-6`).

   List the requests still waiting under "Next step" in `progress.md`, each writer by relationship.

   Done when the person has every draft with its steps and timing, and `progress.md` lists the requests still waiting.

## Check what arrives

7. **Check each recommendation before the person accepts it** (`recommendations-7`). The person pastes the pending text into the conversation. Check it as public text against the front-page test and the targets, and check that the evidence backs each claim, so the person can defend it in an interview. Keep its text in the conversation only (`rules-8`). When it needs changes, draft a short, clean revision note for the person to send with LinkedIn's revision request. The note names each change by where it sits and what it should say instead. Advise a revision over a dismissal, since a dismissed recommendation cannot be retrieved. Tell the person that accepting sends an update to their network.

   Done when the person has your check of each pending recommendation and has decided what to do with it.

8. **Suggest hiding the ones that don't fit** (`recommendations-8`): recommendations on the profile that don't support the target roles, those that claim what the evidence does not back (a team led, a figure, a scope), and those that fail the front-page test, when the writer cannot revise them. Take them from the audit, or check the ones the person pastes as in step 7. The person decides, and hiding can be undone.

   Done when the person has decided about each recommendation flagged.

The procedure is done when each part the person asked for has met its criterion, and no colleague's name is in the workspace.
