## How to use it

The rules live in seven area files, listed under [Areas](#areas). This file holds the index, the platform facts, what LinkedIn does not document, and the `style-` conventions. Each rule has an ID such as `headline-3`, a bold instruction, what the audit checks and what the write phase does, then a Why line and a Source line. The audit checks each row of its checklist against the rules of the areas that row names, and cites the rule IDs behind each judgement. The write phase cites the rule IDs it follows in each draft's Why.

Each rule names its source, the date of that source and its tier. [Sources](sources.md) defines the tiers: tier 1 is LinkedIn's own documentation, tier 2 research that states its method. Help Center pages show only a relative age, such as "Last updated: 2 months ago", so a date written ≈2026-08, or ≈2024 for an older page, is that age counted back from the last full check. "Inference:" marks reasoning beyond what a source says. Quote a figure, such as a percentage, a multiplier or a count, only when a rule gives it, and give its year and source with it. "Verified" describes evidence records only; LinkedIn's own features keep their full names, such as LinkedIn's workplace verification.

## What LinkedIn does not document

These are questions the research could not answer, because LinkedIn's pages are silent or disagree. When the person asks one, say so, and give the skill's answer below instead of a guess. The list follows the order of the areas.

- **Whether AI-assisted profile text or an AI-made headshot needs a label.** The skill gives LinkedIn's advice for posts and leaves disclosure to the person (`rules-5`). For a headshot, it states LinkedIn's likeness rule and leaves the choice to the person (`photo-2`).
- **Whether LinkedIn Learning certificates from an employer-paid seat stay on the profile.** Harvest records the completed courses before access ends (`rules-7`).
- **How much of the headline and About shows before the cut or "see more".** The skill quotes no number and puts the target title and the strongest point first (`headline-3`, `about-1`).
- **What shows below the headline when no position is current.** Every headline variant names the target role (`headline-5`).
- **How Recruiter's Years of experience filter treats gaps, overlaps and positions without dates.** The audit flags positions without a start date, and the person decides how far back Experience goes (`experience-7`).
- **The character limit of a position title or description.** The write phase reports each draft's length, and the person watches the counter in the edit form (`experience-9`).
- **How recruiter search treats a profile whose last position has ended.** The skill ends the position anyway and carries the target title in the headline and the Open to Work job titles (`career-break-1`).

## This skill's own conventions (area `style-`)

These are choices of this skill, not LinkedIn rules and not research findings. Where a LinkedIn source backs part of a convention for About, the row names that rule.

| ID | Convention | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `style-1` | About in the first person; Experience as short lines that start with a verb. `about-7` gives the About half a LinkedIn source. | Reads as the person speaking; verb-first lines are quick to scan. |
| `style-2` | Specific over general: a number, a scope or a tool in each claim; one idea per sentence. In About, `about-5` adds LinkedIn's advice to show each strength with an example. | A specific claim can be checked and remembered; a general one cannot. |
| `style-3` | Plain words; no filler or machine-sounding phrases (the list in `scripts/check_drafts.py`), no em dashes, emoji bullets or hashtags in About. Overused self-descriptions in About fall under `about-5` too. | These patterns make a text read as generated or padded, which costs trust. |
| `style-4` | The verb follows the evidence role in the My role values of [the document format](document-format.md), for example `led` → led, owned; `built` → built, designed; `contributed` → contributed to, worked on; `supported` → reviewed, supported. | Keeps every claim defensible in an interview. |
| `style-5` | Keep the person's own phrasing where it is clear. | The profile should sound like the person who will be interviewed. |
