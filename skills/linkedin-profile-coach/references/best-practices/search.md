# Search

The audit applies these rules to the headline, About, positions, skills, education, location and Open to Work items of its checklist, to its keyword lists, and to Industry, profile completeness and Search appearances; the write phase applies them to the headline, About, experience, skills and settings drafts, to the hand-over and to the final check. The conventions (tiers, the ≈ dates, "Inference:") are explained in [best practices](../best-practices.md).

## Search (`search-`)

The fields and words that LinkedIn search and Recruiter use to find, filter and rank people, used by the audit to check the profile and by the write phase to place keywords.

- `search-1` **Cover the target role's usual qualifications in the postings' words, wherever the evidence backs them.** The audit builds the keep, add and no-evidence-yet keyword lists from `targets.md` (audit step 4) and notes which of the postings' required and preferred qualifications the profile shows evidence for. The write phase places each "add" term in the section where its evidence sits. A term without evidence becomes a question for `work-history-harvest` or stays out.
  - Why: LinkedIn's Talent Blog names job descriptions as good keyword sources. LinkedIn Help advises keywords that accurately reflect your expertise and experience. LinkedIn Recruiter Help says the Keywords filter takes Boolean search strings and searches the member's profile page rather than one field. Recruiter ranks the members who match partly on how similar their work experience and skills are to the search criteria. Advanced AI Search turns a job description into required and preferred qualifications and labels each candidate High (70% or more matched), Medium (30% or more) or Low (less than 30%). It is offered to Recruiter and RPS+ customers with English settings. The Skills filter for applicants focuses on people whose skills meet a high percentage of the job description's requirements. LinkedIn's engineers say Hiring Assistant reads each candidate's profile and resume for evidence that they meet each qualification. Inference: a profile that shows evidence for more of the target role's usual qualifications is more likely to match these searches and to be rated higher.

- `search-2` **Name each key skill in the text where the work shows it, as well as in Skills.** The audit checks that every target skill with evidence appears in the Skills section (`skills-`) and in at least one searchable text field: the headline, About or a position description. It marks `needs work` when a target skill appears only in the Skills list, counts a skill shown only in a Featured item as missing from the text (`featured-3`), and checks each position description against the keywords on the add list that belong to that position. The write phase names each supported skill or tool, with its evidence ID, in the sentence that describes the work, including the line of the position where the person used it, and uses its Skills-list name: the common form that postings and LinkedIn's skill suggestions use.
  - Why: LinkedIn Recruiter Help says a skill search matches skills listed in the Skills section and skills mentioned in the summary, in experience descriptions, in a certificate or course, or in any other section. Recruiter also infers implied skills, for example Data Visualization from Tableau. LinkedIn Help says Skills Match counts the Skills section plus skills it extracts from text in any section, such as the headline, titles, summary and position descriptions. Recruiters see whether an applicant is a Skills Match when they use the Skills match filter. Recruiter Help describes an applicant's Skills Match by listed skills and location only, while member Help also counts skills taken from the text. The two pages may describe different surfaces, so the skill names supported skills in the descriptions as well as in the Skills section, and tells the person that both the list and the text count. LinkedIn's engineers wrote in 2022 that many members mention their most relevant skills in summaries, experience descriptions or resumes instead of the Skills section, so LinkedIn extracts skills from that text to find relevant jobs. LinkedIn Help says job recommendations draw on profile information such as the headline, About, experience and education, and that page does not mention skills. How much the Skills list itself weighs in job recommendations is not public. LinkedIn Help says content in the Featured section is not discoverable through search. That seems to conflict with Recruiter's "any other section", so the skill takes the safe reading and treats Featured as unsearchable. LinkedIn data published in September 2024, from two years of Recruiter searches, showed that 13% of candidate searches performed by recruiters included a skills filter. LinkedIn Help says skills are among recruiters' most common queries. Recruiter's Keywords filter searches the member's profile page. Recruiter then ranks matching candidates partly by how similar their work experience and skills are to the search criteria. Inference: a skill that a recruiter types as a keyword is matched in the profile text, so naming it there helps even without the skills filter. LinkedIn's Search appearances page advises adding relevant keywords to the headline and summary. LinkedIn's engineers wrote in 2025 that Hiring Assistant reads a candidate's profile and resume for evidence that they meet each qualification. Inference: a line that shows the skill in use counts for matching and gives a reader that evidence. The skill still treats titles as the main structured match (`experience-1`). Recruiter's Skills filter offers common skills for the job title the recruiter entered. Inference: the common name is the one recruiters are offered, so it is the safest choice; LinkedIn does not say how it treats other names.
  - Source: [Skills filter in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Skills Match insight on jobs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a793433), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Building LinkedIn's Skills Graph to Power a Skills-First World](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/skills-graph/building-linkedin-s-skills-graph-to-power-a-skills-first-world), LinkedIn Engineering Blog, 2022-11-30, tier 1; [Featured section on your profile FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552452/featured-section-on-your-profile-faqs), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [LinkedIn Data Points Out 2 Simple Ways to Improve Skills-Based Hiring](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/2-simple-ways-to-improve-skills-based-hiring), LinkedIn Talent Blog, 2024-09-03, tier 1; [Order of your profile in people search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Job recommendations based on your preferences and profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-05, tier 1; [AI Search and Projects in Recruiter FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1660341), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Profile search appearances](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1588796), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Building the agentic future of recruiting: how we engineered LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ai/how-we-engineered-linkedins-hiring-assistant), LinkedIn Engineering Blog, 2025-10-21, tier 1.
- `search-3` **Use each keyword in a sentence with its evidence, and vary the terms instead of repeating them.** The audit flags a term repeated across sections without new content, and any keyword with no evidence behind it. The write phase uses each term once or twice per section, in a sentence with its evidence, and a related term where it reads naturally.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says adding more keywords doesn't automatically improve your appearance in search results. It says a profile that appears overly optimized may be affected by spam detection systems, without defining overly optimized. Another Help page suggests using multiple keywords instead of repeating the same words or phrases. It also warns against artificially inflating the Experience and Education fields. LinkedIn's Search appearances page advises adding relevant keywords to the headline and summary (`headline-1`, `about-4`). The skill reads the two pieces of advice together: relevant, varied terms backed by evidence, never repetition for search. The limit of once or twice is the skill's own choice; LinkedIn gives no number.
  - Source: [LinkedIn Search relevance for people search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a524188), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Practices to avoid when optimizing your profile for LinkedIn search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a525117), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Profile search appearances](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1588796), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1.
- `search-4` **Keep the profile location set and current.** The audit checks that a location is set and that it is where the person is based now. The write phase puts any fix on the settings checklist, and target locations, relocation and remote work go into Open to Work (`opentowork-`).
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says members with a location are more likely to be discovered by recruiters. Its job-search best practices say to update your current location. LinkedIn Recruiter Help says the Current location option finds members whose listed current location is the searched place, or who work for a company listed as operating there. Every location search has an implied 100-mile radius. City-level search is not offered for every country, and a search with the Remote workplace type does not use location. For applicants, Recruiter's Skills Match also needs the applicant and the job to be in the same country, except within the European Economic Area (EEA). Inference: once the last role has ended, there is no current employer to match, so the listed location carries the location filter. For other places, LinkedIn Help says that stating the job types and location you want in Open to Work helps your profile show up when recruiters search. Recruiter can limit a location search to candidates who said they are open to relocating there. Its Workplace types filter returns only members who have turned on Open to Work.
  - Source: [Updated location data available to members](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548419), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Find jobs on LinkedIn – Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Search by location in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a417339), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Skills filter in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Filter candidates by workplace type in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a527316), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1.
- `search-5` **Fill the seven sections of LinkedIn's profile level meter before the person applies.** The audit checks for a profile photo, location, industry, education, a position, skills and About (LinkedIn's "summary"). It marks a missing one `missing`, priority high, and cites this rule and the rule of the area that owns the section. The write phase drafts the missing text sections that the evidence supports and lists the others on the settings checklist; `photo-` covers what the photo itself must meet.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says that completing the sections of the profile level meter helps the profile's discoverability in search results and increases profile search appearances. Its guide to a good profile says a complete profile increases discoverability and profile search appearances. LinkedIn gives no data for either claim. The meter gives an All-star rating once seven sections are filled: profile photo, location, industry, education, position, skills and summary. Inference: the summary is the About section. A member reaches Intermediate after four, and only the member sees the level. LinkedIn Help also says that a job application on LinkedIn includes the member's full profile and the account's primary email address. Inference: the profile should be finished before the first application.
  - Source: [Use the profile level meter to update your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a594698), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [How LinkedIn uses your job application information](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507694/save-your-job-application-information), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Create a good LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1.
- `search-6` **Choose the Industry field with the target roles in mind.** The audit checks that the Industry field is set and compares it with the industries in `targets.md`. When the target industry differs from the last employer's, the write phase shows both options, the person picks, and the choice goes on the settings checklist.
  - Why: LinkedIn Recruiter Help says the Industries filter uses the industry a member selects in the Industry field of their own profile. Industry is also one of the seven sections of the profile level meter (`search-5`). Inference: a recruiter who filters by industry finds the person only under the industry they selected. No source says how a career changer should choose, so the skill leaves it to the person.
  - Source: [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Use the profile level meter to update your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a594698), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1.
- `search-7` **Check LinkedIn's job match on a few target postings for missing qualifications.** The audit asks the person for screenshots of job match, or of the "x of y skills match" insight, on two or three target postings; the person takes them, because the skill never opens LinkedIn (hand-paste only). Each missing item joins the add list only when evidence backs it, and otherwise becomes a question for `work-history-harvest`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says job match compares the qualifications from the member's profile and resume with the job's required and preferred qualifications. It advises updating the profile and resume with relevant experiences, skills and certifications to raise the match. Hirers do not see the member's job match level or summary. The older Skills Match insight shows how many of a job's skills match the profile and lets the member add the missing ones. Its Help page is about two years old and says the feature is still rolling out. It is unclear whether job match has replaced the insight, so the skill works from whatever the person sees. Inference: job match also reads the resume, so a change after the new texts may come from either.
  - Source: [Find how you match up with jobs on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7120158), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [Skills Match insight on jobs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a793433), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1.
- `search-8` **Keep updating the profile during the search.** The audit checks that the headline, About, experience and education reflect the person's targets and latest work. At hand-over, the write phase tells the person to bring new evidence, such as a course, a certificate or a freelance project, to another write round when it happens.
  - Why: LinkedIn Recruiter Help says the Active talent spotlight includes members who recently updated their profile or chose to share a resume with recruiters. It shows them as "Engaged in job market" and describes them as more likely to respond to outreach. It does not say how recent the update must be. LinkedIn Help says the jobs it recommends as matching the profile draw on the headline, About, experience, education and location preferences. It advises keeping those sections up to date. Its Search appearances page also advises keeping the profile updated with your latest experience and skills. LinkedIn's User Agreement says an accurate, up-to-date profile makes LinkedIn's recommendations more accurate and relevant, including its recommendations of members to recruiters.
  - Source: [Spotlights in Recruiter, RPS+, and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Job recommendations based on your preferences and profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-05, tier 1; [Profile search appearances](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1588796), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn Legal, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1.
- `search-9` **Read Search appearances for the job titles the person is found for.** The audit asks for the person's screenshot of Search appearances and notes the job titles they were found for, as input to the keyword check.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says Search appearances shows the job titles the member was found for and the top companies and job titles of the people who searched. The data updates weekly, on Wednesdays. The same page advises adding relevant keywords to the headline and summary and monitoring Search appearances after profile updates. It does not say whether LinkedIn Recruiter searches are counted, and it shows no searcher names. Inference: found-for titles far from the target roles suggest that the headline and position titles do not yet use the words people search for.
  - Source: [Profile search appearances](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1588796), LinkedIn Help, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1.
