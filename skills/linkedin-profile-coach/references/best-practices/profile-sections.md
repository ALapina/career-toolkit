# Featured, Other sections, Public profile and URL, Photo

The audit uses these rules for the "Featured", "Custom profile URL and public profile" and "Photo and cover image" rows of its checklist, the rows for Education, certifications, Projects, Languages, contact info, the primary profile button, Services, political signals and profile languages, and the settings named below, and the write phase uses them for `drafts/featured.md`, `drafts/settings.md` and the section entries it drafts. The conventions (tiers, the ≈ dates, "Inference:") are explained in [best practices](../best-practices.md).

## Featured (`featured-`)

The Featured section, where the person pins posts, articles, links and media as work samples; the audit checks it and the write phase drafts `featured.md`.

- `featured-1` **Feature only work the person has the right to publish.** The audit gives `needs work` at high priority, as a confidentiality risk, to any item that shows employer slides, documents, code, designs, internal screens or client names, and the write phase proposes removing it. New items come only from approved evidence records with a public source, such as the person's own posts and articles, public talks, open-source work and personal projects. `featured.md` goes through the NDA pass like every other draft.
  - Why: LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids disclosing information you have no consent to disclose, such as confidential information of others, including your employer (section 8.2, item 5). It also forbids violating others' intellectual property rights, including copyrights, trademarks and trade secrets (item 6). In section 3.1, members promise to share only content they have the right to share. Section 2.5 says others can see, copy and use what members share. The clause says "your employer" and does not mention former employers. Inference: what the person may publish after leaving depends on their own contract, whose terms setup records as extra confidentiality rules.
  - Source: [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1.
- `featured-2` **Fill Featured with public proof of the target skills.** The audit marks Featured `missing` when it is empty and the evidence holds public work that fits the targets, and `needs work` when its items do not fit the target roles. The write phase proposes items in `featured.md`, each with the target skill it shows and its evidence ID.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help presents Featured as the place for the work samples a member is most proud of. It can hold the member's own posts, created or reshared, their LinkedIn articles, links to outside websites, and uploaded images, documents, presentations and videos. Posts from LinkedIn Events or Groups cannot be featured, and an empty Featured section is hidden. LinkedIn's April 2026 guide for graduates advises showing proof of skills by sharing projects, examples of work and the skills built; it is written for new graduates and early-career job seekers. Inference: work samples give the same proof for experienced job seekers.
  - Source: [Manage featured samples of your work on your LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550399/manage-featured-samples-of-your-work-on-your-linkedin-profile?lang=en), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Featured section on your profile FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552452/featured-section-on-your-profile-faqs), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [How Graduates Can Find the Best Job Opportunities in 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/Grads-Guide-2026), LinkedIn Newsroom, 2026-04-15, tier 1.
- `featured-3` **Put Featured keywords in searchable sections too.** The audit flags target keywords and results that appear only in Featured items. The write phase carries them into the headline, About, Experience or Skills drafts, with the same evidence IDs.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says content in the Featured section is not discoverable through search. It also says a viewer must have a LinkedIn account and be signed in to see Featured content. Inference: Featured strengthens the case for people who already have the profile open, but it brings no recruiter to it, and signed-out visitors, such as people arriving from a web search, do not see it.
  - Source: [Featured section on your profile FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552452/featured-section-on-your-profile-faqs), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1.
- `featured-4` **Save copies of the person's own files and links before removing them from Featured.** When `featured.md` proposes removing an item that is the person's own work, it tells them to save the file or link first. For employer-owned material, keeping a copy depends on the person's contract (`featured-1`).
  - Why: LinkedIn Help offers two actions, removing a work sample from Featured only or deleting it, and says a sample removed from Featured cannot be restored. LinkedIn's description of the data download lists shared photos, videos and documents only as URL links and has no category for Featured items. Inference: the person's own copy is the only reliable backup of an uploaded file.
  - Source: [Manage featured samples of your work on your LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550399/manage-featured-samples-of-your-work-on-your-linkedin-profile?lang=en), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Download your data](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339364), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.

## Other sections (`sections-`)

The profile sections and settings that no other area covers: contact details and the primary email, profile languages, Education, Licenses and certifications, Languages, Projects, Volunteering and other entries with political signals, Services and the primary profile button; the audit checks them and the write phase drafts their entries or lists them in `drafts/settings.md`.

- `sections-1` **Use contact details the person keeps after the job ends.** The audit checks the email and websites shown in Contact info; a work address the person will lose gets `needs work`, at high priority. The primary email itself is `settings-1`, and who can see it is `settings-6`. The write phase lists the changes in `drafts/settings.md`: the address shown, and up to three website links, such as a portfolio or a public code profile.
  - Why: LinkedIn's job-search best practices tell members to add contact information if they want to be contacted outside LinkedIn. LinkedIn Help says only 1st-degree connections see the primary email by default; the member can hide it, or show it to 2nd-degree connections or to anyone on LinkedIn. When a member applies for a job on LinkedIn, LinkedIn includes the primary email and the full profile. LinkedIn recommends keeping at least one personal and one work address on the account, so the member can always get in, and only a confirmed address can replace the primary one. Contact info can show up to three website links. Recruiter Help says a recruiter can email a candidate whose address is on the profile instead of sending an InMail. Inference: a work address stops reaching the person once they leave, on the profile and in their LinkedIn applications.
  - Source: [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Manage who can see your email address on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a523134/visibility-of-your-email-address-on-linkedin), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [How LinkedIn uses your job application information](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507694/save-your-job-application-information), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Manage your email address](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a519904), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Add or remove a website from your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548010), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Send messages in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a412220), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
- `sections-2` **Make English the primary profile language when the target roles are advertised in English.** The audit records the primary profile language and any other-language versions, from the person or a screenshot, and compares them with the postings in `targets.md`; when the postings are in another language, it says so and the person decides, since the skill drafts only in English. When the primary profile is in another language, the write phase proposes the English drafts as the primary profile, and warns that re-adding an endorsed skill in English permanently deletes its endorsements.
  - Why: Recruiter's Profile languages filter selects members by the language their profile is written in. LinkedIn Help says each viewer sees the version in their own site language, or the primary profile when no such version exists. Recommendations can be requested only in the primary profile language; for another language, LinkedIn says to change the primary profile language. A skill cannot be switched to another language: it has to be removed and added again, which permanently deletes its endorsements. Inference: an English primary profile is the surest match for a recruiter who filters for English profiles, and every viewer whose site language has no version of its own sees it.
  - Source: [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Create or delete a profile in another language](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541878), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Request a recommendation](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a546682), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025-11, tier 1; [Skill endorsements](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a565106), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1.
- `sections-3` **List each school and degree in Education, and link the skills each one supports.** The audit checks that Education lists the person's schools and degrees and that each entry links the skills the evidence ties to it; an empty Education section falls under `search-5`. The write phase drafts the entries and the skills to link to each, from the evidence.
  - Why: LinkedIn's job-search best practices tell members to update their education. LinkedIn Help advises keeping education current, together with the headline, About and experience, to get better job recommendations. Recruiter's Schools filter reads the schools listed in Education. An Education entry can carry skills or media that support the qualification, and LinkedIn lets members connect skills to education, jobs and projects.
  - Source: [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Job recommendations based on your preferences and profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-05, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Add, edit, or delete education on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a542784), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Available LinkedIn Skill Assessments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507734), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1.
- `sections-4` **List the person's licenses, certifications and courses, with the skills each one proves.** The audit compares the section with the credentials in the evidence and asks about any that no record backs. The write phase drafts each entry as the evidence records it, with the skills to tag.
  - Why: Recruiter Help says a search by skill also matches skills that a member mentions in a certificate or course, or in any other section, not only in the Skills section. LinkedIn's notice retiring its Skill Assessments says members can tag skills to the credentials they hold. LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies forbid misleading information about qualifications and bar promoting fake certifications. A randomized study in South Africa, published in 2022, found that giving job seekers skill-assessment results they could credibly share with employers raised their employment and earnings; results that were hard to share had smaller effects on employment and earnings (Carranza and others, American Economic Review). Inference: a credential an employer can check may count for more than a self-description; this was not tested on LinkedIn.
  - Source: [Skills filter in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Available LinkedIn Skill Assessments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507734), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), LinkedIn, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1; [Job Search and Hiring with Limited Information about Workseekers' Skills](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20200961), American Economic Association (American Economic Review), 2022-11, tier 2.
- `sections-5` **List each working language under Languages, with an honest proficiency level.** The audit compares the Languages section with the languages the person speaks, checks that each language the target postings ask for is listed when the person speaks it, and asks the person to confirm each level, which stays self-reported. The write phase drafts the list.
  - Why: Recruiter's Spoken languages filter lets recruiters filter candidates by the languages they speak and by proficiency level. It is separate from the Profile languages filter (`sections-2`). Several selected languages combine with OR, and one proficiency level applies to all of them. The page does not name the profile section the filter reads. Inference: the filter most likely reads the Languages section, so a language missing there may not match. Members add that section from the Additional group of the Add profile section menu. LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies forbid misleading information about qualifications.
  - Source: [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Add sections to your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a540837/add-sections-to-your-profile), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), LinkedIn, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1.
- `sections-6` **Describe projects by the person's own part, in clean text, with public links only.** The audit checks each Projects entry against the front-page test: no internal project or system names, no client names, no employer documents or code. The write phase drafts entries from approved evidence records, with the verb each record's role allows (`style-4`), tags the skills each one shows, and links only public work, such as an open-source repository or a published talk.
  - Why: LinkedIn lets members connect skills to specific projects as well as to jobs and education, and Recruiter Help says a search by skill matches skills a member mentions in any section of the profile. LinkedIn's April 2026 guide for graduates, written for early-career job seekers, advises showing proof of skills through projects and examples of work. The User Agreement forbids disclosing an employer's confidential information and violating intellectual property rights (section 8.2, items 5 and 6). LinkedIn Help says the Project URL field is not available for new projects, so a link goes in through Add media, then Add a link.
  - Source: [Available LinkedIn Skill Assessments](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507734), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Skills filter in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [How Graduates Can Find the Best Job Opportunities in 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/Grads-Guide-2026), LinkedIn Newsroom, 2026-04-15, tier 1; [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1; [Add sections to your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a540837/add-sections-to-your-profile), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1.
- `sections-7` **Keep other-language versions in step with the English profile, or delete them.** The audit asks whether the profile has versions in other languages and lists the sections in them that no longer match the English drafts. The write phase drafts only the English text and, at hand-over, tells the person to translate and update the other versions on desktop, or to delete the ones they will not keep up.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says changes to the primary profile are not copied to other-language versions and must be made in each one. LinkedIn does not translate them. These versions can be created and managed only on desktop, not in the mobile app. Recruiter's Profile languages filter selects members by the language their profile is written in. Viewers whose site language matches a version see that version, and other viewers see the primary profile (`sections-2`). Inference: an outdated version shows old text, such as the old job, to everyone who uses LinkedIn in that language.
  - Source: [Edit your secondary language profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a570288), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2022, tier 1; [Manage your profile Experience section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a593695), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Create or delete a profile in another language](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541878), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
- `sections-8` **Let the person decide whether Education shows graduation years.** The audit notes which years Education shows and gives the person the facts below as information; it never marks either choice `needs work`. The skill never removes or changes dates on its own.
  - Why: Recruiter's Year of graduation filter uses the end date entered for each school in Education. The page does not say how schools without dates are handled. Inference: a school without an end date may drop out of searches that use this filter. A resume correspondence study of over 40,000 job applications found evidence of age discrimination in hiring against older women, especially those near retirement age, and considerably less evidence against older men (Neumark, Burn and Button, NBER working paper, 2015, revised 2017). A resume audit study published in 2018 found that younger and older applicants got fewer callbacks than prime-aged ones (Farber and others, NBER working paper). Neither study page names the country. Both studies measured age discrimination in hiring, not the effect of showing graduation years on a profile.
  - Source: [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Is It Harder for Older Workers to Find Jobs? New and Improved Evidence from a Field Experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21669), National Bureau of Economic Research, 2015-10, revised 2017-11, tier 2; [Whom Do Employers Want? The Role of Recent Employment and Unemployment Status and Age](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24605), National Bureau of Economic Research, 2018-05, tier 2.
- `sections-9` **Point out political affiliations on the profile, and leave the choice to the person.** When the inputs show Volunteering, Organizations, Causes or Featured entries that name a political party or campaign, the audit tells the person, as information only, and `audit.md` notes that such entries exist without naming the party. The status does not depend on the person's choice, and the skill never judges their views.
  - Why: In a French online experiment published in 2025, 350 adults recruited through social media rated a fictional LinkedIn profile; 57.14% had some recruiting experience. Where the profile showed a party affiliation through volunteer work, 56 of 267 raters (about 21%) gave political reasons when asked why they would or would not hire the applicant.
  - Source: [Evaluating Potential Political Bias in Recruitment: Effects of Extreme vs. Moderate Party Affiliation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10869-025-10096-7), Springer (Journal of Business and Psychology), 2025-12-19, tier 2.
- `sections-10` **Create a Service Page only when the person wants freelance or contract work.** The audit checks Services only when `targets.md` includes freelance or contract work. The write phase then drafts the list of services from the evidence.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says any member can create a Service Page for free, and that Service Pages are aimed at freelancers and small business owners. A Service Page makes the member's services more discoverable on LinkedIn and through search sites such as Google. Services sit either on the member's profile or on a Company Page. Inference: a Service Page helps a job search only when the targets include freelance or contract work.
  - Source: [LinkedIn Service Pages FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a569534), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1.
- `sections-11` **Make Connect the primary profile button, unless the person mainly wants followers.** The audit asks the person which state the Followers setting shows (on means Follow, off means Connect), since their own view of the profile does not show the button others see, and gives Follow `needs work`, at low priority. The write phase adds the toggle to `drafts/settings.md`; the skill never proposes turning on creator mode, whose toggle LinkedIn removed.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says a toggle in the Followers settings makes Follow the primary action for people viewing the profile; turning it off shows Connect, and a change can take up to 24 hours. LinkedIn's notice on creator mode said it would remove the creator mode toggle in March 2024, that the Connect button on profiles would change to Follow, and that the primary action would remain Follow unless the member updated it. Recruiter's Have company connections spotlight picks out candidates who are 1st-degree connections of employees at the recruiter's organization. By default only 1st-degree connections see a member's primary email (`sections-1`). Inference: a visible Connect button makes it easier for people at target companies to become 1st-degree connections.
  - Source: [Manage who can follow you settings](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528011), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Updates to Creator Mode](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a5999182), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Spotlights in Recruiter, RPS+, and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Manage who can see your email address on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a523134/visibility-of-your-email-address-on-linkedin), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.

## Public profile and URL (`url-`)

The public version of the profile that signed-out viewers and search engines see, and the custom profile URL; the audit checks the settings and the write phase lists changes in `drafts/settings.md`.

- `url-1` **Keep the public profile on during the search.** The audit asks for a screenshot of the public profile settings (on desktop, the edit icon next to Public profile & URL on the profile; in the mobile app, Settings, then Visibility, then Edit your public profile). It gives `needs work`, at high priority, when the public profile is off, and otherwise notes whether its main sections are shown. The write phase lists in `drafts/settings.md` which sections the public profile shows, as the person chooses.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says people who are not signed in see the public profile, including people who find the member through a web search, and that search engines such as Google, Yahoo!, Bing and DuckDuckGo can show it. The member chooses which sections it shows, and visitors may need to sign in to see the full profile. Turning it off hides the profile from non-members and from search results. It could also stop the member from recovering the account after losing access to the primary email, because LinkedIn may need the public profile URL to verify them. In a US study of 61 HR professionals in Atlanta and Raleigh, interviewed from 2014 to 2016, most said they reviewed applicants' information on LinkedIn, followed by Facebook profiles and Google searches (McDonald and others, Socio-Economic Review). Inference: with the public profile off, such a Google search does not find the person's profile. Inference: a recruiter who opens the profile link on a resume without signing in also sees the public profile. Inference: the recovery risk matters most while the primary email is a work address the person is about to lose (`sections-1`).
  - Source: [LinkedIn public profile visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a518980), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Control your public LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528138), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Settings for profile photo visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545557/settings-for-profile-photo-visibility), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [The hunt for red flags: cybervetting as morally performative practice](https://academic.oup.com/ser/article/20/3/915/6132113), Oxford University Press (Socio-Economic Review), 2021-02-10, tier 2.
- `url-2` **Set a custom profile URL based on the person's name.** The audit asks for the profile URL and gives `needs work` when it uses neither the person's name nor their professional brand. The write phase proposes two or three name-based options within LinkedIn's limits and adds the steps to `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help recommends a variation of the member's name or professional brand for the custom URL. The custom part can be 3 to 100 characters long, with no spaces, symbols, special characters or the word LinkedIn. It is not case sensitive. URLs go first come, first served, and a member has one custom URL at a time. On desktop the URL is changed through the edit icon next to Public profile & URL on the profile; in the mobile app, through Contact info. LinkedIn is testing a new Profile menu, so the starting point can look different.
  - Source: [Manage your public profile URL](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a542685), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Updates to LinkedIn navigation](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a11028010), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `url-3` **Change the custom URL once, and update links to the old one within six months.** The write phase agrees the final URL with the person before they change it, and lists where the old one appears, such as the resume, an email signature or a portfolio.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says the custom URL can be changed up to five times within six months; after the fifth change, it cannot be changed for another six months. After a change, the previous URL stays linked to the member for six months unless they unlink it, and an unlinked URL can be claimed by someone else after seven days. Inference: links to the old URL keep reaching the person during those six months, so that is the window for updating them.
  - Source: [Manage your public profile URL](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a542685), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `url-4` **Warn that search engines may take weeks or months to show the changes.** The skill says this at hand-over, and again when the person reports old text in web search results.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says search tools such as Google, Yahoo and Bing may take several weeks or even months to reflect changes to a public profile, and that LinkedIn does not control when they refresh.
  - Source: [Control your public LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528138), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
- `url-5` **Leave Off-LinkedIn visibility on during the search, unless the person prefers to limit it.** The audit asks for the state of the setting, under Settings & Privacy, then Visibility, at low priority. The write phase records the person's choice in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says this setting decides whether permitted outside services, such as email integrations, social media aggregators and talent and lead managers, can show the member's profile information to their users. It does not change how search engines show the public profile. Inference: talent management tools are among the places where recruiters work, so leaving the setting on widens the person's reach during a search.
  - Source: [Off-LinkedIn visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340507), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-07, tier 1.

## Photo (`photo-`)

The profile photo and the cover image, checked only against LinkedIn's stated requirements, content rules and job-search advice, never for how the person looks; the audit checks them and the write phase lists changes in `drafts/settings.md`.

- `photo-1` **Add a recent profile photo.** The audit marks the photo `missing` when there is none and asks when it was taken, since a photo does not show its date (`can't tell` until the person answers). The write phase lists a new photo in `drafts/settings.md` when the photo is missing or more than a few years old. Whether to show a photo stays the person's choice.
  - Why: LinkedIn's job-search best practices tell members to add a recent profile photo, and LinkedIn's Talent Blog defines recent as within the past few years. LinkedIn Help says posting a photo is optional and helps connections and others recognize the member. The profile level meter counts the photo as one of its seven sections (`search-5`). LinkedIn Help also says members with a photo get up to 2X more profile views and 3X more connection requests, and gives no method or date. LinkedIn's Talent Blog gives a different figure, also without a method, so the skill quotes only the Help Center figure, as LinkedIn's own claim. In a US interview study of 61 HR professionals (2014 to 2016), the authors write, citing earlier research, that some HR professionals see the lack of a professional picture as a red flag.
  - Source: [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [10 Tips for Taking a Professional LinkedIn Profile Photo](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/tips-for-taking-professional-linkedin-profile-pictures), LinkedIn Talent Blog, 2024-08-01, tier 1; [Settings for profile photo visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545557/settings-for-profile-photo-visibility), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Use the profile level meter to update your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a594698), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [The hunt for red flags: cybervetting as morally performative practice](https://academic.oup.com/ser/article/20/3/915/6132113), Oxford University Press (Socio-Economic Review), 2021-02-10, tier 2.
- `photo-2` **Use a photo that reflects the person's own likeness, however it was made.** The audit gives `needs work` to a photo that LinkedIn may remove and that shows no person at all: one made up only of a company logo, emoji, landscape, animal, words or a flag. Whether a photo of a person is a recent likeness of the member is the person's own statement, never the agent's judgement of the face: the audit asks, and records their answer. When the person asks about an AI-made or AI-edited headshot, or says theirs is one, the skill states the rules below and leaves the choice to them.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says the profile photo must reflect the member's likeness, and allows an illustration, caricature or other artistic rendering of oneself. It lists the photo types above, and other people's likenesses such as stock images, celebrities and fictional characters, as photos it may remove. The Professional Community Policies say not to use an image of someone else, or any image that is not your likeness, as a profile photo. Neither page mentions AI-generated or AI-edited photos. The policies' only rule that touches synthetic media bars media that shows a person saying or doing something they did not, unless its fake or altered nature is clearly disclosed. LinkedIn Help says viewers can see Content Credentials, which can state whether AI was used to make or edit the content, on images and videos that carry C2PA information; it does not say whether profile photos show them. Inference: an AI-made or AI-edited headshot meets LinkedIn's stated rules as long as it still reflects the person's likeness.
  - Source: [Profile photo guidelines and conditions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1377087), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), LinkedIn, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1; [Content credentials](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6282984), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `photo-3` **Set the photo's visibility to Anyone, and to Public on the public profile.** The audit asks for the photo visibility setting, from a screenshot or the person's description, and gives `needs work` when the photo is limited to connections, the network or signed-in members. The write phase lists the change in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says the photo can be shown to 1st-degree connections only, the member's network, all LinkedIn members or anyone, and the default is anyone. The public profile has its own setting, whose widest option is Public, and Public cannot be chosen while the public profile is off (`url-1`). A change made from the profile page also applies to the public profile. When a member shares Open to Work with all LinkedIn members, LinkedIn adds an #OpenToWork frame to the photo; the `opentowork-` rules cover that choice. Inference: a photo limited to connections is hidden from recruiters outside the network, and so is any frame on it.
  - Source: [Settings for profile photo visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545557/settings-for-profile-photo-visibility), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `photo-4` **Upload a PNG or JPG photo within LinkedIn's size limits.** The write phase gives the limits (at most 8 MB, between 400 x 400 and 7680 x 4320 pixels) when the person prepares a new photo or an upload fails.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help gives these limits for profile photos and says GIF files are not supported. LinkedIn's Talent Blog gives the same size range and the same 8 MB maximum.
  - Source: [Photo won't upload to your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a549049), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [10 Tips for Taking a Professional LinkedIn Profile Photo](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/tips-for-taking-professional-linkedin-profile-pictures), LinkedIn Talent Blog, 2024-08-01, tier 1.
- `photo-5` **Use a cover image the person may publish, in the recommended size.** The audit gives `needs work` to a cover image that shows internal screens, documents or client names, which fail the front-page test, and, at low priority, to one with a former employer's branding. The write phase suggests a photo the person took or may use, as a JPG or PNG file under 8 MB, ideally 1584 x 396 pixels.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help recommends a cover image of 1584 x 396 pixels, as a JPG or PNG file under 8 MB. It says photos look better than images with logos, and that the display varies with browser size and screen resolution. It also says an uploaded cover image must comply with the User Agreement, which forbids disclosing an employer's confidential information and violating trademarks and other intellectual property rights (section 8.2, items 5 and 6). LinkedIn's photo guidelines say a background photo may be removed, like a profile photo, under the User Agreement and the Professional Community Policies. Inference: a former employer's branded banner suggests a tie that has ended, so the skill proposes replacing it.
  - Source: [Add or change the cover image on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a568217), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1; [Profile photo guidelines and conditions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1377087), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1.
