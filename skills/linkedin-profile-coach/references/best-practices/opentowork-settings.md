# Open to Work and Settings

The audit uses these rules in the checklist rows "Open to Work", "Contact info and primary email" and "Account, privacy and job-search settings", and the write phase uses them in `drafts/settings.md`. The conventions (tiers, the ≈ dates, "Inference:") are explained in [best practices](../best-practices.md).

## Open to Work (`opentowork-`)

These rules cover switching Open to Work on, its preferences, who sees it and keeping it current; the audit checks them from the person's screenshots, and the write phase drafts the job titles, locations and steps into `drafts/settings.md`.

- `opentowork-1` **Turn on Open to Work with an audience that reaches recruiters, once the new texts are live.** The audit reads the Open to Work box on the profile, or Jobs > Preferences > Open to work, and records whether it is on and which visibility it has. The write phase lists switching it on in `drafts/settings.md` as a step after the person has pasted the new headline, About and latest position.
  - Why: LinkedIn's job search best practices say to turn on #OpenToWork to let recruiters know you are open to new opportunities. Open to Work has three visibility options, and two of them reach recruiters: All LinkedIn members and Recruiters only. The third, Visible only to you, only tailors the jobs LinkedIn shows you. Recruiter Help says members who share Open to Work appear in Recruiter's Open to work spotlight, also when they share with recruiters only. The workplace type filter in Recruiter returns only members who are open to work. A recruiter who has already sourced the person into a Recruiter project gets a notification when the person turns Open to Work on; the page does not say whether Recruiters only triggers it. Inference: switching it on after the paste sends those recruiters to the new texts.
  - Source: [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [View candidates who are open to work in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a419131), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Filter candidates by workplace type in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a527316), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1.
- `opentowork-2` **Fill every Open to Work preference from `targets.md`, with job titles written for recruiters.** The audit compares the job titles, location types, locations, start date and employment types on the Open to Work screen with `targets.md`. The write phase drafts them in `drafts/settings.md`: standard titles for the target roles that agree with the headline, every workplace type and employment type the person would accept, only locations the person is happy to show, and a start date that matches their availability.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help lists these preferences: job titles, location types, locations, start date, employment types and visibility. It says that stating the types of jobs you want and your preferred location helps your profile show up when recruiters search. LinkedIn Help also says standard job titles are what members search for. Two Help pages disagree on what others see. One says Recruiter users see the open flag, the job titles you want, whether you want full-time or contract work, and the date you flagged yourself as open. The other says sharing makes your location and location type visible to others and keeps the rest private. The skill drafts the titles as if recruiters read them and treats the locations as visible. Recruiter Help says the workplace type filter in Recruiter returns only members who are open to work. When a recruiter searches for remote work, location is not used, so results may be global. Members can also flag interest in contract work for Recruiter's employment type filter. Inference: each workplace type or employment type left out takes the person out of searches filtered on it. LinkedIn also builds job recommendations from these preferences and from the headline, About, experience and education. The Help pages give no limit on the number of titles or locations. Members in India can also add notice period and expected annual salary, and LinkedIn Help says only recruiters see these two, whatever the visibility. Recruiter Help offers an "Open to relocate" filter but does not name the member setting behind it, so the skill treats relocation as unconfirmed.
  - Source: [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Privacy for shared career interests](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a510407), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [Job recommendations based on your preferences and profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-05, tier 1; [Order of your profile in people search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Filter candidates by workplace type in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a527316), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1; [Search by location in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a417339), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Identify candidates open to contract work in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a416314), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
- `opentowork-3` **Explain the three audiences, then let the person choose.** The audit reads the Visibility field on the Open to Work screen and checks the top-of-profile screenshot for the #OpenToWork photo frame, which shows only with All LinkedIn members. The write phase explains each option in one sentence, records the person's choice in `drafts/settings.md`, and flags a frame that does not match it.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help defines the audiences. All LinkedIn members means everyone on LinkedIn, including recruiters and people at your current company, and it adds the #OpenToWork frame to the profile photo. Recruiters only means people who use LinkedIn Recruiter, LinkedIn's paid recruiting product. Recruiter Help says these members appear in Recruiter's Open to work spotlight without the frame, and that Recruiter Lite lacks this spotlight. Visible only to you sends no signal and only tailors the jobs LinkedIn shows you. With Recruiters only, LinkedIn tries to hide the signal from Recruiter users at your current company and at related companies. It takes the current company from the position marked "I am currently working here" and says it cannot guarantee complete privacy. No LinkedIn page says whether recruiters at a former employer are excluded once the last position has an end date, and the skill tells the person this is unknown. LinkedIn recommends no audience. The only LinkedIn figures on the frame found for this reference date from 2020 and state no method, and no study with a stated method on how recruiters react to the frame was found. The skill therefore presents the choice without figures. Inference: All LinkedIn members reaches more people, including the person's network and hirers without a Recruiter seat; once the job has ended, the former employer already knows the person has left, so the choice is mostly reach against privacy.
  - Source: [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Privacy for shared career interests](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a510407), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [View candidates who are open to work in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a419131), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Spotlights in Recruiter, RPS+, and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Job recommendations based on your preferences and profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-05, tier 1.
- `opentowork-4` **While Open to Work is on, answer every recruiter InMail, even with a short no.** The audit asks whether recruiter InMails are waiting for a reply and whether LinkedIn has emailed to ask if the person is still open to work. On request, the write phase drafts a short yes reply and a short no reply for the person to send themselves.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says that if you stop responding to InMails from recruiters, LinkedIn emails you to confirm you are still open to work. Without a confirmation, it removes Open to Work until you turn it on again. Recruiter Help mentions a reminder after two unanswered recruiter InMails in a row. Recruiter ranks search results partly on how likely a candidate is to respond, and LinkedIn does not list what feeds that estimate. In an October 2020 blog post, LinkedIn advised accepting recruiter messages or clicking "no thanks" and said this helped members rank higher in recruiters' searches. Recruiter search has changed since, so the skill cites that post for the advice, not for a ranking effect. Inference: a short reply keeps Open to Work on, and it may count toward the response estimate.
  - Source: [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [View candidates who are open to work in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a419131), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [AI Search and Projects in Recruiter FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1660341), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [To Find Your Next Job More Quickly, Tell Your Community You're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/member/product/the-benefits-of-sharing-with-your-community-you-re-open-to-work), LinkedIn Official Blog, 2020-10-29, tier 1.
- `opentowork-5` **Change Open to Work by editing it, and switch it off when the person takes a new job.** When the person reports a new job, the audit checks that Open to Work is off. The write phase adds to `drafts/settings.md` how to change it later: the Edit icon on the Open to Work box, and the photo's Frames menu to drop the frame but keep the recruiter signal.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says Delete switches the feature off and does not keep your previous preferences. The photo's Frames menu offers two choices: remove Open to Work from the profile, or share it with recruiters only so other members no longer see it. Recruiter Help says LinkedIn prompts members to turn the signal off when they start a new position.
  - Source: [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Change Your Profile Photo Frames](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a566495), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [View candidates who are open to work in Recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a419131), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1.

## Settings (`settings-`)

These rules cover the settings that decide how recruiters and employers find, reach and read the person, and LinkedIn's limits on applying; the audit checks them from screenshots the person takes, setup raises the two that depend on the work mailbox, and the write phase lists each change in `drafts/settings.md` for the person to make by hand.

- `settings-1` **Make a personal address the account's primary email.** Screen: Settings & Privacy > Sign in & security > Email addresses; look for the address marked primary and record whether it is a work or a personal address, never the address itself. When it is a work address, setup raises it at once, and the write phase puts "add a personal address, confirm it, make it primary" first in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says a job application on LinkedIn includes your account's primary email address and your full profile. LinkedIn also emails the link to your data download to the primary address. LinkedIn Help recommends adding at least one personal and one work address so you can always access your account, and only a confirmed address can become primary. It warns that turning off the public profile (`url-1`) could stop you recovering the account if you lose access to the primary email. Inference: once the job ends, the work mailbox closes, and replies to applications, the data download and account emails sent there are lost.
  - Source: [How LinkedIn uses your job application information](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507694), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Export connections from LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a566336), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025, tier 1; [Manage your email address](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a519904), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [LinkedIn public profile visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a518980), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `settings-2` **Keep InMail on, and turn on Open Profile when the person has Premium.** Screen: Settings & Privacy > Data privacy > Who can reach you > Messages; look for "Allow others to send you InMail?" set to Yes. For a Premium subscriber, also look for the Open Profile toggle in Edit intro, next to the Premium badge. The write phase lists any change in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: Recruiter Help says Recruiter users can InMail any member in their search results except members who opted out of InMail. Another Recruiter Help page says recruiters may then find no way to message the member at all. LinkedIn Help names the member's toggle. With Open Profile on, other members can message a Premium subscriber for free, without InMail, but free accounts can send only a limited number of these messages a month. Recruiter Help shows messages to Open Profile members as free for the recruiter. Inference: InMail on keeps recruiters able to write, and Open Profile lets more hirers write at no cost.
  - Source: [Search in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415399), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1; [Send messages in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a412220), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Opt Out of Receiving InMail Messages](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554229), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Manage your Open Profile settings as a Premium subscriber](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541684), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025, tier 1.
- `settings-3` **Add LinkedIn's workplace verification while the work email still works.** Screen: the person's profile, where LinkedIn shows their verifications; look for a workplace verification, and ask the person when the screenshot does not show one. Setup raises it while the person still has the work mailbox. The write phase records in `drafts/settings.md` whether it is done and, once the work email is gone, points to identity verification with a government ID where LinkedIn offers it.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says workplace verification by work email needs an address on your current company's email domain and is available only at select companies. The code it sends expires 15 minutes after it arrives. LinkedIn may show a job seeker's verifications to users of its enterprise products, for example in Recruiter's spotlights. Recruiter's Verifications spotlight lists applicants with verifications such as workplace, school or government ID. LinkedIn's newsroom says Hiring Assistant 2, rolling out to English-language customers from November 2026, shows recruiters a candidate's verification status. LinkedIn reports more profile views for members with a verification, without a method or a comparison group: "close to 90% more" in a September 2026 newsroom post and "60% more" on a Help page. Recruiter Help tells hirers not to count a missing verification against a candidate, so the skill presents verification as one trust signal among several. No LinkedIn page says what happens to a workplace verification after the person leaves. Inference: the address must match the current company, so the person completes the verification before the last position gets an end date and before the mailbox closes.
  - Source: [Workplace verification with your work email](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1423367), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Verifications on your LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1359065), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1; [Spotlights in Recruiter, RPS+, and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [LinkedIn expands verification to help members and businesses build trust on and off LinkedIn](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/linkedin-launches-new-verification-features-to-build-professional-trust), LinkedIn Pressroom, 2026-09-23, tier 1; [LinkedIn Announces Hiring Assistant 2, its Next Generation Hiring Agent, to Help Recruiters Deliver Stronger Hiring Outcomes](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/hiring-assistant-2), LinkedIn Newsroom, 2026-09-29, tier 1.
- `settings-4` **Switch on sharing the profile when applying, once the new texts are live.** Screen: Settings & Privacy > Data privacy > Job seeking preferences; look for "Share your profile when you click Apply for a job" and read its state, because LinkedIn does not document the default. The write phase lists the switch in `drafts/settings.md` among the steps after the paste.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says that with this setting on, LinkedIn shares your full profile with the job poster as soon as Apply takes you off LinkedIn, for example to a company website. The job page then shows a banner with an Undo link. Applications made on LinkedIn include the account's primary email and the full profile, and Easy Apply may add relevant skills from the profile. LinkedIn's privacy FAQ says it sends no update to your connections or your employer when you view or apply for a job. Inference: every application carries the profile as it stands that day, so the setting helps most once the new texts are live; the person can still apply before then.
  - Source: [Sharing your full profile with job posters on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512339), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [How LinkedIn uses your job application information](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507694), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Apply to jobs directly on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512348), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025, tier 1; [LinkedIn Privacy FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/privacy/faqs?lr=1), LinkedIn Legal, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1.
- `settings-5` **Keep a current English resume on LinkedIn, and share its data with recruiters.** Screen: Jobs > Preferences > Resumes and application data; look for at least one current resume, any outdated ones, and the "Share resume data with recruiters" toggle (a newer page calls it "Share resume data with hirers") switched on. The write phase adds the steps to `drafts/settings.md` and asks the person to put each saved resume through the front-page test, because recruiters can search its content.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says that with resume sharing on, your profile appears when recruiters search for skills, experience, education and accomplishments found in the resumes you saved over the past two years. It needs at least one saved resume and works only in English. Recruiters do not get the full resume unless you give it to them, for example by applying. LinkedIn tries to hide the data from recruiters at your current company but cannot guarantee complete privacy. Recruiter's skills search can use the resume details members share, and its Active talent spotlight lists members who chose to share a resume. LinkedIn recommends Word or PDF files under 2MB. A resume added to the profile as media can be downloaded by anyone who views the profile and cannot be used to apply. LinkedIn does not share saved resumes and application answers with employers automatically, and you can edit them before you submit. LinkedIn may use saved resumes to recommend and rank jobs and to improve its AI models, and the person can opt out of saving them. A Help page from about 2024 says the prompt after an application offers resume sharing with the toggle already on; no current page confirms this, so the audit reads the toggle. Inference: an older saved resume stays searchable for two years, so the person deletes saved resumes that are out of date or would fail the front-page test.
  - Source: [Share and manage your resumes with recruiters](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1327213), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Skills filter in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Spotlights in Recruiter, RPS+, and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Upload your resume to LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a508383), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [How LinkedIn uses your job application information](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507694), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Visibility and usage of your uploaded resume](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a506429), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025, tier 1.
- `settings-6` **Choose with the person who can see their email address.** Screens: the Contact info section of the profile and Settings & Privacy > Visibility > Visibility of your profile & network > Email visibility. Look for a personal address (`settings-1`) and its visibility level, and record the level, never the address. The write phase records the person's choice in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn's job search best practices say to add contact information if you want to be contacted outside LinkedIn. By default only your 1st-degree connections see your primary email. The options are "Only visible to me", 1st-degree connections, 1st and 2nd-degree connections, and anyone on LinkedIn. In Recruiter, when a candidate's email is on their profile, the recruiter can send an email instead of an InMail. Inference: wider visibility lets recruiters outside the network write directly, and the person weighs that against unwanted mail.
  - Source: [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Manage who can see your email address on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a523134), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Send messages in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a412220), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
- `settings-7` **Tell each target company that the person wants to work there.** The audit marks these signals `can't tell` unless the person reports them. The write phase lists up to 50 target companies from `targets.md` in `drafts/settings.md`, in priority order, each with three actions: click I'm interested on the company Page's About or Life tab, follow the Page and engage with its posts, and connect with people the person already knows there.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says the I'm interested button privately tells a company you want to work there, even when it has no open roles. Only hirers at that company see the signal, and it expires after a year. You can signal up to 50 companies, LinkedIn suggests choosing them in priority order, and some companies do not have the button. Recruiter's Interested in your company spotlight, in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite, lists members who clicked I'm interested. It also lists members who follow the Page or like, share or comment on its posts. Its Have company connections spotlight lists 1st-degree connections of the company's employees. LinkedIn's job search best practices also say to follow target companies' Pages and engage with their content. Inference: a connection with someone the person knows there puts them in that second spotlight, and the I'm interested signal needs renewing after a year if the interest lasts.
  - Source: [Signal interest in working for a company](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1380509), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-01, tier 1; [Spotlights in Recruiter, RPS+, and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1.
- `settings-8` **Set job alerts for the target titles, places and companies.** Screen: Jobs > Preferences > Job alerts; look for an alert for each target title and location, and company alerts for the top target companies. The write phase lists the alerts in `drafts/settings.md`, 20 at most: job searches to run with the alert toggle on, and company alerts to create from each Page's Jobs tab.
  - Why: LinkedIn's job search best practices say to set up job alerts based on previous searches, preferences and specific companies. The same page suggests finding remote jobs and setting up a job alert to increase your chances of being one of the first applicants. LinkedIn Help says you create an alert by running a job search and switching on the job alert toggle, and a company alert from the Jobs tab of the company's Page. A new alert defaults to daily, by email and LinkedIn notification, and can be switched to weekly. A member can have at most 20 alerts at once. Creating an alert can fail when settings block LinkedIn emails or when the maximum is reached.
  - Source: [Find jobs on LinkedIn - Best Practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a509392), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-03, tier 1; [Job alerts on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a511279), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-04, tier 1; [Manage job alerts](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1420165), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Create job alerts for specific companies](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554166), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2022, tier 1; [Troubleshoot job alerts](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1426166), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2022, tier 1.
- `settings-9` **Apply by hand and at a steady pace, within Easy Apply's limits.** No screen shows this; the write phase adds the limits to `drafts/settings.md`. When the person asks about auto-apply bots or mass-application tools, the skill explains LinkedIn's ban on automation (`rules-1`) and these limits.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says it sets daily and speed limits on Easy Apply, partly to curb automation and bots. It does not publish the daily number, and the limit resets at the end of the day (UTC). Sending many applications in a short time triggers a temporary pause on Easy Apply. LinkedIn may lower a member's daily limit if it notices activity that seems inauthentic.
  - Source: [Easy Apply limits](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a8068422), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025, tier 1; [Apply to jobs directly on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512348), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025, tier 1.
- `settings-10` **Switch off profile-update notices before the person pastes anything.** Screen: Settings & Privacy > Visibility > Visibility of your LinkedIn activity > Share profile updates; look for it switched off. The write phase lists it in `drafts/settings.md` as a step before the paste, tells the person to switch off the "Share with network" or "Notify network" toggle in each position form they edit, and notes that they can switch notices back on for news they want shared, such as a new job.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says this setting decides whether your network hears about your job changes, education changes and work anniversaries. LinkedIn's privacy FAQ says profile edits are shared with your network unless you turned notifications off. Changes to the introduction section, which holds the headline, do not notify the network. Help pages also describe a toggle in the edit forms, which `experience-8` covers. One calls it "Share with network" and offers it while you edit your profile on a computer. Another puts a "Notify network" toggle at the top of the Add experience page. A third says the network may be told of Experience changes when Notify Network is on. The skill treats headline edits as silent and position edits as announced unless the toggle is off, and asks the person to check the live form. The setting controls notices only: anyone who opens the profile sees each edit as soon as it is saved. LinkedIn does not itself advise switching notices off while editing. Inference: with notices off, a rewrite of many sections does not reach the network as a run of separate updates.
  - Source: [Share profile updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a529062), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1; [LinkedIn Privacy FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/privacy/faqs?lr=1), LinkedIn Legal, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1; [Edit the introduction section on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a547248), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [Update your current position in your introduction section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550169), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Manage your profile Experience section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a593695), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1.
- `settings-11` **In the EEA or Switzerland, let the person choose whether Jobs uses their wider LinkedIn data.** Screen: Settings & Privacy > LinkedIn Services, a screen for members there; look for whether Jobs is connected. The write phase records the person's choice in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says members in the EEA and Switzerland choose whether to connect Jobs with their core LinkedIn experience. Connecting lets LinkedIn link the data and give more tailored job recommendations. Without it, services may seem less tailored, but the number of jobs shown stays about the same. For members who made no choice before March 6, 2024, LinkedIn stopped sharing data between these services.
  - Source: [Connecting to LinkedIn Services](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6222119), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-07, tier 1.
- `settings-12` **On a free account, show name and headline when viewing profiles, if the person wants their viewer list.** Screen: Settings & Privacy > Visibility > Visibility when viewing other profiles; look for the viewing mode. The write phase records the person's choice in `drafts/settings.md`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says a Basic (free) account sees who viewed its profile only when its own viewing mode shows name and headline. Nobody, even with Premium, sees the names of people who viewed in private mode. Inference: with the viewer list on, the person can see which recruiters looked at the profile and follow up.
  - Source: [Who's viewed your profile visibility settings](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a568195), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
