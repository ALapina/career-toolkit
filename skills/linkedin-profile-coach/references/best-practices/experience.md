# Experience and Career break

The audit uses these rules for the checklist rows on each position and on gaps and the end of the last role, and the write phase uses them for the position drafts and the career break entry; `career-break-4` also covers how the last role ended, when About mentions it. The conventions (tiers, the ≈ dates, "Inference:") are explained in [best practices](../best-practices.md).

## Experience (`experience-`)

These rules cover each position's title, employer and company Page, dates, description and match with the resume; the audit uses them for the checklist item on each position, and the write phase for the position drafts. For the skills and tools each description names, see `search-2`; for the skills linked to each position, see `skills-3`.

- `experience-1` **Use a standard job title that names the role and its level accurately.** The audit compares each title with the titles in the target postings and flags internal, creative, abbreviated or vague titles, and titles whose level differs from the one in `progress.md` or the evidence records. The write phase keeps an official title that is already standard. Otherwise it proposes the closest standard title of the same function and level, asks the person to confirm it and to check it against their resume (`experience-6`), and names the official title in the description.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says standard job titles may be boring, but they are what other members search for. LinkedIn's 2020 advice to job seekers is to use a standard job title that recruiters would search for, such as "Data Analyst" rather than "Data Ninja". Recruiter's Job titles filter reads the titles members add in the Experience section, and the recruiter can set it to current titles, past titles or both. Recruiter derives its Seniority categories from job titles, and its Seniority filter uses the level of the position at the member's current company. Its Job functions filter reads the titles of all present positions. Recruiter Help adds that filters match standardized versions of profiles, while keyword search matches a term anywhere on the profile. LinkedIn does not say how it maps a nonstandard title. Inference: an unusual title can miss the Job titles, Seniority and Job functions filters at once, and the words recruiters type. Inference: once the last role has an end date, its title matches only searches set to past titles or both, and the Seniority and Job functions filters may not match it at all. LinkedIn does not say which setting recruiters use most. LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies forbid misleading or deceptive information about work experience. Inference: a standard title stays accurate only when it names the same function and level as the official one, and the official title in the description keeps both visible. The headline carries the target title (`headline-1`), and the end date stays accurate (`career-break-1`).
  - Source: [Order of your profile in people search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [To Find Your Next Job More Quickly, Tell Your Community You're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/member/product/the-benefits-of-sharing-with-your-community-you-re-open-to-work), LinkedIn Official Blog, 2020-10-29, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [AI Search and Projects in Recruiter FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1660341), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), LinkedIn, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1.
- `experience-2` **Give each title held at one employer its own position.** The audit compares the positions in the export with the titles in `progress.md` and the evidence records. It flags a promotion or role change that is missing or folded into one position. The write phase drafts one position per title, each with its own dates, and tells the person to set the employment type on each.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says it groups positions at the same company under one company section when a new position starts within one month of the previous position's end date. The section shows the company name and logo once, with the total tenure. LinkedIn Help advises specifying the employment type for each position to show career progression within a company. When positions are grouped wrongly, it says to check and update the company name. Recruiter's Job titles filter can match past titles as well as current ones. Inference: a separate position keeps the earlier title searchable and shows the promotion.
  - Source: [Position grouping in your profile's experience section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548226), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2025-11, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1.
- `experience-3` **Link each position to its employer's LinkedIn Page.** The audit checks each position's company name and Page link in a screenshot of the Experience section, and marks the item `can't tell` without one. It flags a missing link, a misspelled or outdated company name, and a wrong Page. The write phase tells the person which employer's Page to link; for contract work, it names the agency or the person's own business and leaves clients unnamed, as the Confidentiality section of `SKILL.md` requires.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help tells members to associate themselves with the correct LinkedIn Page. Recruiter's Companies filter reads the Company Name field of the Experience section and can match current or past employers. Recruiter Help says filters match standardized versions of profiles. Inference: a position linked to the employer's Page matches the Companies filter more reliably than free text. LinkedIn does not say how it maps company names that have no Page. LinkedIn Help lists company name, title, job description, location and employment dates as the Positions data in the data download. Inference: the download does not show the Page link, so the audit needs a screenshot.
  - Source: [Representing Yourself Accurately on Your Profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1337379), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2022, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [AI Search and Projects in Recruiter FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1660341), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [Download your data](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339364), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `experience-4` **List only real professional experience with each named organization.** The audit flags placeholder positions, positions at organizations the person was only a customer or user of, and titles or dates that conflict with the evidence records or the employment facts in `progress.md`. The write phase takes titles, employers and dates from approved evidence records, the employment facts in `progress.md`, and export entries the person confirms.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says associations in the Experience section must be accurately labeled and must reflect professional experience with the named organization. Its examples are employment, contract work, an official internship, volunteer experience and board service. It says informal associations, such as being a customer or user of an organization's products or services, should not be included. LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies forbid associating yourself with an organization you are not professionally associated with. They also forbid misleading or deceptive information about your work experience. LinkedIn Help says untruthful information could lead other members to flag the profile as suspicious, which could result in account restrictions. Another Help page asks members to avoid artificially inflating the Experience fields. Inference: a placeholder position such as "Open to work" names no organization the person works with, so the time since the last job goes in a career break entry instead (`career-break-2`).
  - Source: [Manage your profile Experience section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a593695), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), LinkedIn, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1; [Representing Yourself Accurately on Your Profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1337379), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2022, tier 1; [Practices to avoid when optimizing your profile for LinkedIn search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a525117), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1.
- `experience-5` **Describe each position by the results the evidence supports.** The audit marks a description that lists only duties as `needs work` when approved records hold results for that position, and an empty description as `missing`. The write phase opens with one line on the scope of the role, such as the team, the product area and the main tools. Then it gives the results from approved records, strongest first, each citing its evidence ID, with the person's own figures and none of the employer's business figures.
  - Why: The research found no LinkedIn page and no study with a stated method that compares results with lists of duties in Experience descriptions. For the About section, LinkedIn's Talent Blog advised in 2024 citing the biggest takeaway from the experience section, with lines such as "I've made X ... in the past X years". It praised an example summary for including numbers as proof of achievements. Inference: the same advice fits each position's description. LinkedIn's engineers wrote in 2025 that Hiring Assistant reads a candidate's profile, resume and other relevant data for evidence that the candidate meets each qualification. Inference: a result stated with its context is that kind of evidence. LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids disclosing confidential information of others, including your employer. The clause does not mention former employers. For them the person's own contract decides, so the skill applies the clean rules of `SKILL.md` either way.
  - Source: [14 LinkedIn Profile Summaries That We Love (And How to Boost Your Own)](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/linkedin-profile-summaries-that-we-love-and-how-to-boost-your-own), LinkedIn Talent Blog, 2024-04-30, tier 1; [Building the agentic future of recruiting: how we engineered LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ai/how-we-engineered-linkedins-hiring-assistant), LinkedIn Engineering Blog, 2025-10-21, tier 1; [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1.
- `experience-6` **Keep titles, employers and dates the same as on the resume.** When the person provides their current resume, the audit compares title, employer and dates for every position and lists each difference. The write phase changes a title in both places or in neither, and tells the person which resume lines to update.
  - Why: A 2022 review of cybervetting research, which also draws on the authors' interviews with 77 hiring agents in two US metro areas from 2014 to 2016, names checking whether LinkedIn or Google information matches the resume as a common practice.
  - Source: [Is cybervetting valuable?](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S1754942622000281/type/journal_article), Cambridge University Press (Industrial and Organizational Psychology), 2022-09-09, tier 2.
- `experience-7` **Agree with the person how far back the Experience section goes.** The audit notes the earliest start date and flags any position without a start date. It lists older positions that do not fit the targets as a question for the person. The write phase explains the trade-off below in two sentences and drafts only the positions the person keeps.
  - Why: Recruiter's Years of experience filter counts from the start date of the earliest position in the Experience section, whatever its industry or company. LinkedIn does not say how it treats gaps, overlaps or positions without dates. LinkedIn Help advises making sure you can be found by your current and past positions, schools and field of study. Inference: dropping early jobs or internships can move the person into a lower experience range. LinkedIn Help also advises keeping the profile updated with the professional experiences that align with current career goals. Inference: early positions can also reveal the person's age. A field experiment with over 40,000 job applications, published in 2015 and revised in 2017, found evidence of hiring discrimination against older women, especially near retirement age, and considerably less against older men. A 2018 resume audit study found that younger and older applicants got fewer callbacks than prime-aged ones. Neither abstract names the country. The two effects pull in opposite directions, so the person decides.
  - Source: [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Order of your profile in people search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2023, tier 1; [Create a good LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1; [Is It Harder for Older Workers to Find Jobs? New and Improved Evidence from a Field Experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21669), National Bureau of Economic Research (Working Paper 21669), 2015-10 (revised 2017-11), tier 2; [Whom Do Employers Want? The Role of Recent Employment and Unemployment Status and Age](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24605), National Bureau of Economic Research (Working Paper 24605), 2018-05, tier 2.
- `experience-8` **Set the network notification on purpose for each position change.** The write phase asks, for each position the person adds, ends or changes, whether their network should hear about it. It tells the person to set the notification toggle in the edit form before saving. This is a write-phase step; the account-wide setting is in `settings-10`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says that if the Notify Network option is on, the network may be informed of changes to the Experience section. Another Help page says a Share with network toggle sets whether a change made on a computer notifies the network. LinkedIn's privacy FAQ says profile edits are shared with the network unless notifications are turned off. The pages name the toggle differently, and none gives its default. Inference: a position change can be announced unless the toggle is off, so the person checks the live form.
  - Source: [Manage your profile Experience section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a593695), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1; [Share profile updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a529062/share-profile-updates-with-your-network), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-08, tier 1; [LinkedIn Privacy FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/privacy/faqs?lr=1), LinkedIn, undated, read 2026-10-30, tier 1.
- `experience-9` **Check title and description length against the counter in LinkedIn's edit form.** LinkedIn documents no character limit for either field. The write phase reports each draft's length, quotes no fixed limit, and asks the person to watch the form's counter when pasting. Length is not an audit item.
  - Why: LinkedIn's API position schema on Microsoft Learn states no maximum length for the title or the description. LinkedIn's Help page on the Experience section gives none either. So the skill relies on the counter in the edit form.
  - Source: [Profile Position Reference Schema](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/references/v2/profile/position), LinkedIn API documentation on Microsoft Learn, 2026-04-30, tier 1; [Manage your profile Experience section](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a593695), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2024, tier 1.

## Career break (`career-break-`)

These rules cover how the profile shows the end of the last job, the time since, how that job ended, interim jobs and older gaps; the audit uses them for the checklist item on gaps and the end of the last role, and the write phase for the last position and the career break entry.

- `career-break-1` **End the last position with its end month and year once the job has ended.** The audit compares the last position with the employment period in `progress.md`; a position still shown as current after the job ended is `needs work`, priority high. The write phase puts the end month and year in that position's draft, tells the person to clear "I am currently working here", and points to the notification toggle in `experience-8`. While the person is still employed, the position stays current, and the next step in `progress.md` reminds them to end it after their last day.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help tells members to update their profile each time they change jobs. In LinkedIn's position data, a position without an end month and year is current. When a super admin of a company Page with LinkedIn's Page verification reports a member's current employment as inaccurate, the member has 7 days to verify their work email. Otherwise the position is disassociated from the Page across LinkedIn. For a report through a Page without that verification, LinkedIn removes the reported information within 14 days unless the member challenges it. Inference: once the job has ended, the person can usually no longer verify the old work email, so an accurate end date is what keeps the position from being reported as current employment. LinkedIn's User Agreement says an accurate, up-to-date profile makes its recommendations more accurate and relevant, including recommendations of members to recruiters. Its job recommendations page asks members to keep the experience section up to date. Ending the position has a cost. Recruiter's Job titles filter can be set to current titles, past titles or both. Its Seniority filter uses the position at the current company, and its Job functions filter uses present positions. Inference: once ended, the last title matches only searches that include past titles, and it may drop out of the Seniority and Job functions filters. LinkedIn does not say which setting recruiters use, or how it treats members with no current position. The skill ends the position anyway, because LinkedIn asks for an accurate, current profile. It carries the target title in the headline and the Open to Work job titles instead (`headline-1`, `opentowork-2`). LinkedIn Help says that naming the kinds of jobs and the location wanted in Open to Work helps the profile show up when recruiters search. LinkedIn also takes the position marked "I am currently working here" as the current company when it hides Open to Work from that company's recruiters. LinkedIn does not say whether that hiding still covers a former employer once the position has an end date, so the skill tells the person this is unknown (`opentowork-3`).
  - Source: [Representing Yourself Accurately on Your Profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1337379), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2022, tier 1; [Profile Position Reference Schema](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/references/v2/profile/position), LinkedIn API documentation on Microsoft Learn, 2026-04-30, tier 1; [Inaccurate information on another member's profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1337291), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1; [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1; [Job recommendations based on your preferences and profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-05, tier 1; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Let recruiters know you're Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-10, tier 1.
- `career-break-2` **Offer a career break entry for the time since the last job ended.** The audit checks whether a career break entry or a real current position covers that time; if neither does, the status is `missing`, priority medium, and the person decides whether to add one. The write phase drafts the entry for the person to add under Add profile section, Core, Add career break: the closest type the form offers, or none, and a description written to `career-break-3`. Real current work, such as freelance projects, goes in as a position instead, under `experience-4`.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says a career break is an entry in the Experience section, added from Add profile section under Core. The member can pick a type, or leave it blank and add details in the description section. Logged-in members can see the entry. LinkedIn does not notify the network when it is added. The page does not say whether signed-out visitors see it. It was last updated about five years ago, so the person checks the live form. LinkedIn launched career breaks in March 2022 with 13 options, including caregiving and health and well-being. LinkedIn does not document the current list of types. In 2022 LinkedIn also said breaks would soon show up in Recruiter searches. No current Recruiter Help page in the research confirms it, so the skill tells the person that recruiters may not be able to filter on a break. LinkedIn reported in 2022 that 51% of employers would more likely call a candidate back if they knew the context of a career break. It also reported that one in five hiring managers reject candidates with breaks outright. The post mentions a survey of nearly 23,000 workers and more than 7,000 hiring managers but gives no method. Inference: once the last position has an end date, any viewer can see how long the gap has lasted. Research on whether a longer gap costs callbacks is mixed. A US field experiment published in 2012 found that callbacks fell as the spell grew, with most of the drop in the first eight months. In a 2014 Swedish field experiment, employers penalized current spells of at least nine months but not shorter ones. A 2016 audit study of experienced college-educated women applying for administrative support jobs found no link with duration. A 2018 resume audit study covering a broad age range found fewer callbacks at 52 weeks and no link among shorter spells. Neither of the last two abstracts names the country. No study tested a career break entry, so the person decides.

Evidence from a Field Experiment](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.104.3.1014), American Economic Association (American Economic Review), 2014-03, tier 2; [Determinants of Callbacks to Job Applications: An Audit Study](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.p20161010), American Economic Association (American Economic Review: Papers & Proceedings), 2016-05, tier 2; [Whom Do Employers Want? The Role of Recent Employment and Unemployment Status and Age](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24605), National Bureau of Economic Research (Working Paper 24605), 2018-05, tier 2.
- `career-break-3` **Describe the break by what the person is doing and learning.** The audit flags a break description that is empty, apologizes or undersells the person. The write phase drafts a few short, factual lines from approved evidence records: the courses, certifications, freelance work or volunteering of the break, each with the skills it covers.
  - Why: LinkedIn Help says details of a career break go in its description section. In LinkedIn's 2022 survey, which gives no method, 52% of hiring managers said candidates should bring up a career break in the interview and highlight what they learned. In the same survey, 50% said people returning from a break have often gained valuable soft skills, and 46% said candidates undersell them. Asked about the biggest mistake candidates make when discussing a break, 48% of hirers named undervaluing themselves on pay or seniority. Inference: the description should say plainly what the person did and learned, without apology. A 2024 study of US LinkedIn profiles from 2015 to 2021, by authors employed at LinkedIn, found that skills added during an employment break had a substantially stronger relationship with shorter gaps than skills already listed. The data are observational and do not show cause. Recruiter's skill search counts skills a member mentions in any section, including certificates and courses. Inference: each course or certificate in the description should name the skills it covers.
  - Source: [Add Career Breaks to Your Profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a597655/add-career-breaks-to-your-profile?lang=en), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2021, tier 1; [A new way to represent career breaks on LinkedIn](https://news.linkedin.com/2022/march/new-way-to-represent-career-breaks-on-linkedin), LinkedIn Newsroom, 2022-03-01, tier 1; [Skill Signals in a Digital Job Search Market and Duration in Employment Gaps](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12122-024-09363-y), Springer Nature (Journal of Labor Research), 2024-10-16, tier 2; [Skills filter in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2024, tier 1.
- `career-break-4` **Say how the last role ended at most once, in neutral words.** The audit checks About, the rest of the profile and anything the person writes for the drafts; it flags criticism of the former employer, its managers or their decisions, apology, repeated mentions, and details that fail the front-page test, such as changes to other people's jobs or internal reorganizations. The write phase asks whether the person wants the profile to say how the role ended. If they do, it drafts one plain sentence for the career break entry, the last position or About, with details that concern other people or the employer only when the employer has made them public; in About, the next sentence moves on to what the person offers.
  - Why: In a study published in 2021, 61 HR professionals interviewed in two US metro areas from 2014 to 2016 treated online blog and status posts in which candidates criticized their employers as a red flag. The study covered posts, not profiles. Inference: profile text that criticizes a former employer, About included, reads the same way when a recruiter looks the candidate up. LinkedIn's 2022 survey found that one in five hiring managers reject candidates with career breaks outright. No source in this reference shows how hirers judge a sentence on a profile about how a job ended. Inference: one plain sentence gives the context without dwelling on it. LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids disclosing confidential information of others, including your employer. The clean rules of `SKILL.md` allow the plain fact of how the person's own role ended and leave out internal reorganizations and changes to other people's jobs.
  - Source: [The hunt for red flags: cybervetting as morally performative practice](https://academic.oup.com/ser/article/20/3/915/6132113), Oxford University Press (Socio-Economic Review), 2021-02-10, tier 2; [LinkedIn Members Can Now Spotlight Career Breaks on Their Profiles](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/linkedin-members-spotlight-career-breaks-on-profiles), LinkedIn Talent Blog, 2022-03-01, tier 1; [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), LinkedIn, effective 2025-11-03, tier 1.
- `career-break-5` **Let the person decide whether to list an interim job.** The audit treats an accurate interim position as `good` and leaves it in place. The write phase gives the person the evidence below in two or three sentences. It drafts the position, with an accurate title and dates, only when the person wants it listed.
  - Why: The evidence conflicts. A 2016 audit study of experienced college-educated women applying for administrative support jobs found that holding a low-level interim job made a callback significantly less likely. A 2018 resume audit study found that applicants working in an interim job when they applied got fewer callbacks than unemployed applicants, whether the interim job was of lower or comparable quality. Neither abstract names the country. In a 2014 Belgian field experiment with male applicants, employers penalized a history of unemployment more than a history of overeducation, that is, of working in a job that needs less education than one has. Whether any of this carries over to software, QA, analyst or product roles is untested. Recruiter's Seniority filter uses the position at the current company, and its Job functions filter uses present positions. Inference: an interim job listed as current also sets the level and the job function that those filters read. LinkedIn Help advises keeping the profile updated with the experiences that align with current career goals. So the skill takes no side, and the person decides.
  - Source: [Determinants of Callbacks to Job Applications: An Audit Study](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.p20161010), American Economic Association (American Economic Review: Papers & Proceedings), 2016-05, tier 2; [Whom Do Employers Want? The Role of Recent Employment and Unemployment Status and Age](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24605), National Bureau of Economic Research (Working Paper 24605), 2018-05, tier 2; [Unemployment or Overeducation: Which is a Worse Signal to Employers?](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/8312), IZA Institute of Labor Economics (Discussion Paper 8312), 2014-07, tier 2; [Recruiter and Recruiter Lite search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428), LinkedIn Recruiter Help, updated ≈2026-09, tier 1; [Create a good LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351), LinkedIn Help Center, updated ≈2026-02, tier 1.
- `career-break-6` **Let the person decide whether an older gap gets a career break entry.** The audit lists the gaps between earlier positions with their dates and asks the person which, if any, to explain. The write phase drafts a career break entry for an older gap only when the person asks for one.
  - Why: A 2014 Swedish field experiment found that past long-term unemployment did not affect hiring decisions. The authors put this down to later work experience removing the negative signal. In a 2014 Belgian field experiment, employers penalized a history of unemployment more than a history of overeducation. The countries and designs differ, so the evidence is mixed and the person decides.
  - Source: [Do Employers Use Unemployment as a Sorting Criterion When Hiring? Evidence from a Field Experiment](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.104.3.1014), American Economic Association (American Economic Review), 2014-03, tier 2; [Unemployment or Overeducation: Which is a Worse Signal to Employers?](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/8312), IZA Institute of Labor Economics (Discussion Paper 8312), 2014-07, tier 2.
